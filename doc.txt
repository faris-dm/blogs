project


— a small community feed app

The pitch (keep it simple on purpose): Users sign up, follow other users, post short text/image posts, like and comment on posts, and see a feed of posts from people they follow. Think a stripped-down Twitter/Instagram feed — no need for stories, DMs, or algorithms. The simplicity of the idea is intentional — I don't want you burning time on scope, I want you burning time on getting the hard parts right.

Why this project, specifically — the hard parts hiding inside it
The feed query itself — "show me posts from people I follow, newest first, paginated" is a deceptively hard SQL problem once you have real data volume. This forces you to actually think about indexes, N+1 query bugs, and pagination — the stuff that makes apps "feel slow" in real life.
Likes — this is a race condition disguised as a checkbox. Two rapid clicks on "like" (sound familiar?) can create duplicate likes if you don't design the database correctly. Same disease as the duplicate order bug, different symptom.
Follow/unfollow — ownership and authorization again: can I unfollow someone on your behalf by guessing IDs? Can I see a private account's posts by hitting the API directly, bypassing your UI's "follow to see posts" logic?
Comments and posts — full CRUD with the ownership rule baked in: can only the author edit or delete their own post/comment. You will 100% mess this up at least once, and that's fine — that's the point.
Rate limiting — what stops someone scripting 10,000 posts a second? This introduces you to a concept we haven't touched yet, and it's a real production concern.
